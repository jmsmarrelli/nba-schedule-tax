Method notes

What the README leaves out: the specification choices, the things that did not work, and where I think this is thin.

Why fixed effects rather than a rating system

The obvious alternative is to build a team rating (an SRS, a Pythagorean expectation, an Elo) and then regress residuals on schedule variables. That works, but it makes the schedule terms depend on how well the rating system happened to fit, and it introduces a generated-regressor problem in the standard errors that nobody ever corrects for.

Estimating team strength and schedule effects in the same regression avoids both. Team-season effects and schedule terms are identified jointly, and the standard errors come out of the same covariance matrix.

The cost is a 619-column design matrix, which for 23,838 games is a rounding error on a laptop.

Why differenced schedule terms

The restriction is that a burden is worth the same in absolute value to whoever is carrying it: the home team on a back-to-back loses what the away team on a back-to-back loses.

That is testable. symmetry_test() fits home and away burdens as separate coefficients and reports their sum, which the restriction says should be zero:

Nothing rejects at conventional levels. The back-to-back row is the closest, and its direction is worth noting: it hints that a back-to-back may hurt the home team slightly less than the away team, which is plausible — the home team slept in its own bed — but at t = 1.7 across twenty seasons it is not something to build a rotation on.

What did not work

Continuous altitude. Covered in the README. The per-venue fit is in output/altitude_check.csv. The short version: Denver and Salt Lake City show the same bonus despite a thousand feet between them, and nothing near a thousand feet shows anything, so a linear term in feet is imposing a shape the data does not have.

Direction of time-zone travel. I fit eastward and westward shifts separately, expecting the westward-to-eastward direction (losing hours, playing on a body clock that thinks it is later) to be worse. The two coefficients were not distinguishable, so the model keeps the absolute value. With a season of games per team and a maximum of three zones, there is not enough variation to resolve the asymmetry that the sleep-science literature would predict.

Road trip length. The Nth-straight-road-game term comes out slightly positive (+0.11, t = 1.8). I do not believe road trips help. The likeliest explanation is that this term is picking up something about which teams take long trips and when — the annual rodeo road trip in San Antonio, western swings scheduled around building conflicts — that the team-season effect cannot absorb because it varies within the season. It is kept in the model as a control and I would not quote it as a finding.

Attendance. The schedule files carry it. It is close to capacity for most games in most seasons, so there is almost no variation to use, and what variation exists is a function of team quality, which the fixed effects have already taken.

Possessions

The standard estimator: FGA + 0.44×FTA − OREB + TOV, averaged across the two teams because a game's possession count is shared to within one.

The 0.44 is a league-average guess at what fraction of free throw attempts end a possession. Over a full game the error averages out to well under a possession. It would not be acceptable at the level of a stint or a quarter, and this project never uses it at that level.

An alternative is to count possessions directly from the play-by-play. That is more accurate and it costs the twenty-season sample: the play-by-play mirror is missing games in several seasons, while the box scores are essentially complete. For a question about the calendar, having every game matters more than having exact possessions, so this project takes the box score and its sibling project on plus-minus takes the log.

Where I think this is thin

Rest is measured, load is not. Days between games is a proxy for how tired a team is. Minutes played, overtime, blowouts that emptied the bench, and who actually traveled are all invisible here. A team coming off a 25-point win with its starters resting the fourth quarter is not the same as one coming off double overtime, and this model calls them identical.

The back-to-back coefficient is a treated effect. Teams already manage back-to-backs — that is what load management is. So −1.52 is what remains after the mitigation, and it will shrink further as teams get better at it, which means this number is not stable across the sample and I have not tested whether it drifts.

Selection into rest. A team's rest is set by the schedule maker, which is close to exogenous. Which players take that rest is not. The model cannot separate "the team is tired" from "the team sat two starters", and those have opposite implications for what a front office should do about it.

Two buildings. The altitude finding rests on Denver and Salt Lake City. It is the largest coefficient in the model and it has the thinnest identification, and those two facts belong in the same sentence every time it is quoted.
